This guide is written from Medsafe’s pages on [the WAND database](https://www.medsafe.govt.nz/Regulatory/DevicesNew/3WAND.asp), [what WAND is](https://www.medsafe.govt.nz/Regulatory/DevicesNew/3-2Explanation.asp) and [importing medical devices](https://www.medsafe.govt.nz/Regulatory/DevicesNew/4Importing.asp), read on 14 October 2026. It isn’t legal advice.

## No pre-market approval

Medsafe says:

- There is **no approval system** for medical devices under the *Medicines Act 1981*.
- There is **no mandatory requirement** for devices to be approved by any regulator before being supplied in New Zealand.
- Notification to WAND **does not mean** Medsafe has assessed the device for quality, safety or efficacy.

This differs from Australia, where devices must be included on the ARTG, and Canada, where Class II and higher devices need a Health Canada licence before sale.

## What WAND is

The Web Assisted Notification of Devices (**WAND**) database was set up by the *Medicines (Database of Medical Devices) Regulations 2003*.

- Devices must be notified to WAND to be **legally supplied** in New Zealand.
- Notification is **free**, with no ongoing fees.
- It must happen within **30 calendar days** of a person or organisation becoming the device’s sponsor.
- Importers, exporters and local manufacturers must all notify their devices.

## Every importer notifies separately

A WAND notification covers one sponsor and one device. If several companies import the same CPAP machine, **each one must notify it**; one importer can’t rely on another’s notification. Sponsors must keep supply records so they can trace customers in a recall.

## What this means for you

1. Buy from a New Zealand supplier that can tell you it has notified the device.
2. Medsafe uses WAND to contact sponsors when there is a safety problem, so a machine bought overseas may not be traced in a recall.
3. Check Medsafe’s online recalls database for your model.
