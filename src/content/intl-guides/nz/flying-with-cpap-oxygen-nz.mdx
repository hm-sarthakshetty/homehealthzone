This guide is written from Air New Zealand’s [medical equipment and special assistance page](https://www.airnewzealand.com/en-nz/travel-info/special-assistance-medical-equipment), read on 14 October 2026. Confirm with the airline before you fly.

## Airline oxygen

- Complete **Parts 1 and 2 of the MEDA** medical clearance form and send it at least **72 hours** before travel (48 hours if you are using your own POC).
- You **can’t use your own oxygen bottles** on the aircraft.
- **Domestic flights:** Air New Zealand refers you to an authorised oxygen provider where possible.
- **International flights:** Air New Zealand supplies oxygen on board (not on the ground or in transit). The standard supply is 2 L/min by nasal prongs from an Avia Pulse bottle, at **NZD $150 per bottle**. Flow rates above 4 L/min need a fixed oxygen rig at extra cost.

## Your own portable oxygen concentrator (POC)

- There is **no charge** for a POC you bring.
- It must be an **Air New Zealand approved model**, or meet FAA requirements and carry the manufacturer’s label saying so.
- Medical clearance is required to use it in flight.
- Give **48 hours’ notice** to use aircraft power, and check in **1 hour before** general check-in.
- Air New Zealand’s battery-approved list names models including the AirSep FreeStyle, SeQual Eclipse, Inogen One G2v and Philips SimplyGo. Newer models depend on the FAA label.

## CPAP

- Complete **MEDA Part 1**, even if your model isn’t listed, and give **48 hours’ notice**. Check in 1 hour before general check-in.
- Use is limited to the **cruise** phase.
- The approved list on Air New Zealand’s page names older models (ResMed S8 and S9, F&P Icon, Philips Remstar, Transcend). It doesn’t name the AirSense 10 or 11, so ask about your machine.
- **In-seat power** (110 V, 60 Hz, maximum 1.2 A) is approved only for listed CPAPs, some only in low-power mode with the humidifier off.

## Aircraft medical power

| Aircraft | Medical outlet |
| --- | --- |
| Domestic flights | None |
| Boeing 787-9 | 230 V, 50 Hz |
| Boeing 777 | 115 V, 400 Hz; allow at least two weeks for Air New Zealand Engineering to assess your device |

Air New Zealand recommends battery power where possible. If your device doesn’t meet its requirements, it suggests hiring one for the trip; see [oxygen concentrator prices](/nz/guides/oxygen-concentrator-price-nz/) for local hire.

## Power in New Zealand

New Zealand uses **230 V, 50 Hz** with **Type I** plugs (two flat pins in a V plus an earth pin) ([Electrical Safety First](https://www.electricalsafetyfirst.org.uk/safety-advice/travel-advice/new-zealand/)). Visitors from the US or Canada need a plug adaptor. A device rated only for 120 V also needs a converter. An adaptor doesn’t change voltage.
