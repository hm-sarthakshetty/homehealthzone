It is not legal advice.

## How licensing works

Health Canada licenses medical devices under the *Food and Drugs Act* and the *Medical Devices Regulations*. It says:

- MDALL lists **all licensed Class II, III and IV** medical devices offered for sale in Canada.
- **Class I** devices don’t need a medical device licence. They are monitored through establishment licensing.
- **Only products in the listing may be offered for general marketing in Canada.**
- You can search by company name, licence name or number, device name or device identifier (often the catalogue number).

## Checking before you buy

1. Search MDALL for the exact model name.
2. Check the licence has no end date. For example, one Evora nasal mask record under licence 67268 ended on 28 March 2023, while newer Evora records under the same licence remain active.
3. For connected machines, Canadian CPAP Store points out that its AirSense 11 is ResMed’s Canadian model, with a cellular connection certified for use in Canada. A model bought abroad may differ.
