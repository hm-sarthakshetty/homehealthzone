The NHS provides home oxygen free after assessment. Its portable option is small cylinders: the [NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/tests-and-treatments/home-oxygen-treatment/) says most weigh 2–3 kg and hold up to 3 hours of oxygen. A portable oxygen concentrator (POC) makes its own oxygen from room air, so it doesn't run out while the battery lasts. This guide covers buying one privately, for example for flying, longer days out or holidays.

## Pulse dose isn't the same as NHS continuous flow

Both machines above deliver oxygen in pulses as you breathe in, measured in settings rather than litres per minute. A setting number isn't equivalent to a litre-per-minute flow. If your NHS prescription is continuous flow, or you need oxygen while asleep, ask your oxygen team whether a pulse-dose machine suits you. If it doesn't, you need a continuous-flow model.

## VAT relief

[HMRC Notice 701/7](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/reliefs-from-vat-for-disabled-and-older-people-notice-7017) names oxygen concentrators as zero-rated medical appliances. This applies when they are supplied to someone chronically sick or disabled for personal or domestic use. Spare batteries designed solely for the device can also qualify, but general-purpose batteries don't. See our [VAT relief guide](/uk/guides/vat-relief-cpap-oxygen-uk/).

## Flying

The [CAA](https://caa.co.uk/air-passengers/about-your-trip/health-and-medical/travelling-with-medicines-mobility-and-medical-equipment/) says:

- contact the airline before your trip
- carry a doctor's letter or your prescription
- expect the device to go through the X-ray scanner in a separate tray

Most airlines don't provide aircraft power for passenger medical equipment, so plan enough battery for the whole journey. All batteries, including spares, must meet the airline's rules. See [flying with CPAP or oxygen](/uk/guides/flying-with-cpap-oxygen-uk/).

## The verdict

If you fly or travel often and your clinic agrees a pulse-dose setting is right, the Rove 6 with the 16-cell battery (£2,150 before VAT) is the longer-running choice. The iGo2 (£1,995) is lighter. If your NHS prescription is continuous flow, neither replaces it, so speak to your oxygen team before buying.
