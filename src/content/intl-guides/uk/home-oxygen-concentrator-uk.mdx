Most people in the UK should get home oxygen through the NHS. The [NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/tests-and-treatments/home-oxygen-treatment/) assesses you, and a contracted supplier installs, services and supports the equipment. NHS guidance also says the supplier arranges payment for the electricity a concentrator uses. See [how the NHS home oxygen service works](/uk/guides/nhs-home-oxygen-service/).

The NHS warns that buying oxygen online and using it without a prescription can be dangerous. It can damage the brain, heart and lungs, or even kill. If you do buy privately, for example as a backup or for a second home, use only the flow your clinician prescribed.

These figures assume the maximum listed draw, all day. Many machines draw less at low flow. The retailer says the DeVilbiss 525 draws less below 2.5 L/min. On the NHS, the supplier arranges payment for this electricity. If you buy privately, you pay it yourself.

## VAT relief

[HMRC Notice 701/7](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/reliefs-from-vat-for-disabled-and-older-people-notice-7017) lists oxygen concentrators as zero-rated medical appliances for a chronically sick or disabled person buying for personal or domestic use. See [VAT relief on CPAP and oxygen](/uk/guides/vat-relief-cpap-oxygen-uk/).

## 5 or 10 litres?

Buy for your prescription, not the maximum. A 5-litre machine covers prescriptions up to 5 L/min. A 10-litre machine is only needed above that, and it costs more to run, weighs more and is louder: the DeVilbiss 1025 is listed at 57 dBA against 40 dBA for the 525.

## The verdict

Use the NHS route if you need home oxygen. It covers the equipment, servicing and electricity. If you have a specific reason to buy, a 5-litre machine such as the DeVilbiss 525 (£810 before VAT) or VisionAire 5 (£840) covers most prescriptions. Buy a 10-litre machine only if your prescription is above 5 L/min.
