This guide is written from the Australian Energy Regulator's [Energy Made Easy guidance](https://www.energymadeeasy.gov.au/article/customers-using-life-support-equipment), the NSW Government's [Life Support Rebate page](https://www.energy.nsw.gov.au/households/grants-rebates/life-support-rebate) and the [NSW Social Programs for Energy Code](https://gazette.nsw.gov.au/gazette/2026/7/2026-7_307-gazette.pdf), read 14 October 2026.

## Register as a life support customer

Energy Made Easy lists life support equipment as including:

- an oxygen concentrator
- a chronic positive airways pressure (CPAP) respirator
- a ventilator for life support
- any other equipment a doctor says is needed for life support

**How:** tell your energy retailer or your distributor as soon as possible. They send you a form for you and your doctor to complete. Return it to them.

**What you get:** they must not disconnect you for any reason, and must give you at least **4 days' notice** of a planned interruption.

Tell your retailer and distributor if your address or phone number changes.

## Make a power-cut plan

Registration does not prevent unplanned outages. Energy Made Easy says to plan:

- where you will go, and how you will get there with your equipment
- how you will contact people; cordless phones need mains power
- who you will call, with a written list of your doctor, nearest hospital and a helper
- backup equipment, kept fully charged
- where you could get a temporary generator; ask your energy provider whether it can supply one

Queensland's MASS asks every home oxygen client to have a Power Failure Emergency Plan (form MASS 48). See [Victoria and Queensland funding](/au/guides/home-oxygen-funding-victoria-queensland/).

## NSW Life Support Rebate

The rebate helps households where someone uses approved energy-intensive life support equipment at home. A registered medical practitioner must confirm the need and complete the declaration on the application form; you send it to your electricity retailer. Service NSW can help on 13 77 88.

Rates in the Social Programs for Energy Code version 8.4, effective 24 August 2026:

| Equipment | Per day | About per year |
| --- | --- | --- |
| PAP device (CPAP), 24-hour use | $0.7096 | $259 |
| PAP device (CPAP), under 24 hours | $0.3589 | $131 |
| Oxygen concentrator, 24-hour use | $3.1096 | $1,135 |
| Oxygen concentrator, under 24 hours | $1.8493 | $675 |

The rebate is paid per billing day for each approved device, so a household with both a CPAP and a concentrator gets both rates. Eligibility lasts four years from the application date, and you can reapply.

EnableNSW notes its Home Respiratory Program clients may be eligible: see [EnableNSW](/au/guides/enablensw-home-respiratory-program/).

## Other states

Energy Made Easy is run by the Australian Energy Regulator. Rebates outside NSW are set by each state or territory; ask your retailer how registration and rebates work where you live.
