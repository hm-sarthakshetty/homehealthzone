This guide is written from the Medicare Benefits Schedule (MBS) entries for [item 12250](https://www9.health.gov.au/mbs/fullDisplay.cfm?type=item&q=12250) and [item 12203](https://www9.health.gov.au/mbs/fullDisplay.cfm?type=item&q=12203), and ResMed Australia's [guidance on funding](https://www.resmed.com.au/knowledge-hub/are-cpap-machines-covered-by-medicare-or-private-health-insurance), read 14 October 2026.

## The sleep study

### Home study: MBS item 12250

Item 12250 covers an **unattended** adult sleep study, the kind usually done at home.

| | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Schedule fee | $401.25 |
| Medicare benefit at 75% | $300.95 |
| Medicare benefit at 85% | $341.10 |
| Frequency | Once in any 12-month period |

### Lab study: MBS item 12203

Item 12203 covers an **attended** study, with a sleep technician in continuous attendance under a sleep physician's supervision. It applies, for example, where suspected obstructive sleep apnoea makes you unsuitable for an unattended study.

| | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Schedule fee | $703.80 |
| Medicare benefit at 75% | $527.85 |
| Medicare benefit at 85% | $599.30 |

Any gap between what the provider charges and the Medicare benefit is yours to pay.

## Getting referred

Item 12250 can be claimed in two ways:

1. **Screening route.** Your doctor refers you to a qualified adult sleep medicine practitioner or a consultant respiratory physician, who decides you have a high probability of symptomatic moderate-to-severe OSA. That needs an **Epworth Sleepiness Scale score of 8 or more**, plus one of:
   - a **STOP-Bang** score of 3 or more
   - an **OSA50** score of 5 or more
   - a high-risk result on the **Berlin Questionnaire**
2. **Specialist route.** After seeing you, in person or by video, the sleep or respiratory specialist decides a study is needed to confirm the diagnosis.

The study must be supervised and reported by a qualified adult sleep medicine practitioner who reviews the raw data. The MBS notes the questionnaires are available from Sleep Central.

## Paying for the CPAP

The MBS items cover the investigation, not the machine. Funding for the equipment comes from elsewhere:

- **State programs.** ResMed Australia says public funding of CPAP differs between states, each with its own criteria. In NSW, [EnableNSW](/au/guides/enablensw-home-respiratory-program/) supplies CPAP for a $100 annual co-payment for pensioners and lower earners, though waiting periods vary.
- **Private health insurance extras.** ResMed says extras cover may pay a rebate towards a CPAP and sometimes a mask. The amount varies widely; some funds want a doctor's letter, and all need an eligible invoice. Ask your fund for a quote before buying.
- **Buying.** CPAP machines and masks can be sold GST-free; see [GST-free CPAP and oxygen](/au/guides/gst-free-cpap-oxygen-australia/) and [CPAP machine prices in Australia](/au/guides/cpap-machine-price-australia/).

## Renting or trialling first

ResMed's Australian online shop lists an [AirSense 11 AutoSet trial](https://shop.resmed.com.au/products/airsense-11-autoset-cpap-trial) at $35, with a separate ongoing-fee listing. ResMed says the rented machine comes with up to two masks to try and check-ins from its sleep coaches, and asks you to contact its team to book. The listing does not state the billing period, so confirm the full cost before you start.
