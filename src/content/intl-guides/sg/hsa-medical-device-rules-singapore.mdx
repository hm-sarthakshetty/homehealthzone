This guide is written from HSA's pages on [personal import of medical devices](https://www.hsa.gov.sg/travelling-with-medication-and-medical-devices/personal-import/) (updated 30 April 2026) and [medical device registration](https://www.hsa.gov.sg/medical-devices/registration-of-medical-devices/register-your-device/overview/), read 14 October 2026. It is not legal advice.

## Bringing a device in for personal use

**You need HSA approval before you travel.** HSA's rules:

| Rule | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| When to apply | At least **10 working days** before you arrive |
| How | HSA's online form at go.gov.sg/hsamdpersonalimport2 |
| Prescription devices | A letter from your doctor confirming and justifying the need |
| Quantity | Up to **3 months' supply** per approval; more is rejected |
| Repeat shipments | Several shipments of the same device within 3 months are not allowed |
| On arrival | No declaration needed, but carry the documents and approval email for customs checks |
| Leaving | Take the device with you when you leave Singapore |
| Resale | Not allowed, except to the family members the device was for |

**Exceptions:**

- Devices already in use when you arrive, such as implants or devices already in contact with your skin or eyes.
- Transit passengers who stay inside the transit zones.

HSA does not allow personal import of devices meant to be used by a qualified practitioner, radiation-emitting devices for trained staff, or HIV test kits.

### What this means for CPAP and oxygen users

- **Anyone flying in with a CPAP or POC** should apply before travelling, with a doctor's letter. HSA's page does not distinguish visitors from residents, and it reviews each application case by case.
- **Buying overseas** to save money is a personal import too. HSA may refuse it if the import looks commercial.
- **Selling a used machine** that came in this way is not allowed.

## Devices sold in Singapore

Commercial suppliers face a different regime. HSA classes medical devices from **Class A** (lowest risk) to **Class D**.

- **Class A** devices are exempt from product registration, but the importer or manufacturer must notify HSA through its SHARE system.
- **Class B, C and D** devices must be registered with HSA. HSA offers faster evaluation routes for devices already approved by reference regulators overseas, on the same labelled use.

HSA's registration pages do not give a class for CPAP machines or concentrators, so ask the retailer whether the model is registered with HSA.

## The ACE subsidy and approved distributors

The government's CPAP subsidy names its supply route: the ResMed AirSense 10 AutoSet through Junnimed Service Pte. Ltd. or Easmed Pte Ltd. See [CPAP subsidy in Singapore](/sg/guides/cpap-subsidy-singapore/).
