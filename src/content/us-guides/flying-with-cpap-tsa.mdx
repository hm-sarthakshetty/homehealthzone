Checked 13 October 2026. Airline policies vary; check yours.

## TSA screening: what each page says

| Question | TSA FAQ | TSA item page (updated 15 Jul 2026) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Allowed in carry-on? | Yes | Yes |
| Remove from its case? | Yes, for X-ray | May stay in case; may need to come out of the carry-on |
| With TSA PreCheck | Not stated | Can stay in its case and in the carry-on |
| Lithium-battery devices | Not stated | Should be carried on |

The two pages disagree on the case. Pack so the device comes out easily, and follow the officer's instructions.

## On the plane (AirSense 11 example)

| Item | What ResMed says |
| --- | --- |
| Carry-on allowance | Medical devices don't count toward the carry-on limit |
| In-flight use | Meets FAA requirements; air travel compliance letters at resmed.com |
| Humidifier | Must be empty, but the tub or side cover must be inserted or the device won't run |
| Airplane mode | Switch on when airline staff require it |
| Aircraft power | Power supply rated 115 V, 400 Hz, 1.5 A for aircraft use |

Other models have their own documents; check yours before you fly.

## Packing checklist

- Device, mask, tubing and power supply in the travel bag (ResMed advises using it to prevent damage)
- Humidifier tub emptied and packed separately
- The right power cord for your destination
- The airline's compliance letter, printed
- Batteries in your carry-on ([CPAP battery options](/us/guides/cpap-battery-camping-power/))

## Verdict

Carry your CPAP on, empty the humidifier, and be ready to take it out of the case at screening.
