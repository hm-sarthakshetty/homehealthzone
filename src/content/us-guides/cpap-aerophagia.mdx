Waking up bloated, burping or with stomach pain after a night on CPAP has a name: aerophagia, literally "air swallowing". The AASM describes it as air passing into the throat and stomach, producing "bloating, belching, and/or flatulence" ([AASM PAP troubleshooting FAQ](https://sleepeducation.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/PAP-FAQs.pdf)).

## What the guidance says to try

| Approach | Source | Who changes it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Treat nasal congestion | AASM | You and your clinician |
| Try a full face mask, which allows mouth breathing | AASM | You and your supplier |
| Switch from a face mask to nasal pillows or a nasal mask | ATS | You and your supplier |
| Change sleep position and/or number of pillows | ATS | You |
| Use expiratory pressure relief | ATS | You (on many machines) |
| Lower overall pressure, or expiratory pressure on BiPAP | AASM | Your clinician |
| Ask whether auto-PAP or bilevel suits you better | ATS | Your clinician |
| Over-the-counter or prescribed gas/bloating remedies | AASM | You and your clinician |

Sources: [AASM PAP troubleshooting FAQ](https://sleepeducation.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/PAP-FAQs.pdf), [American Thoracic Society tip sheet](https://site.thoracic.org/advocacy-patients/patient-resources/pap-therapy-quick-tips-for-troubleshooting-to-address-problems-with-use).

The two societies point in opposite directions on mask type: the AASM suggests a full face mask, the ATS suggests nasal pillows or a nasal mask. Which helps depends on how the air is getting in, so try one change at a time and note the result.

## Comfort settings you can usually change yourself

On the ResMed AirSense 11, two comfort settings are in **My Options** ([ResMed AirSense 11 user guide](https://document.resmed.com/documents/products/machine/airsense-11/user-guide/airsense11_user-guide_amer_mul.pdf)):

- **Expiratory Pressure Relief (EPR).** "When EPR is enabled, you may find it easier to breathe out." Your care provider must have enabled it on the machine.
- **Ramp.** Starts at a lower pressure and rises to your prescribed pressure over Off, 5–45 minutes, or Auto. That helps you fall asleep, but it doesn't change the pressure later in the night.

## Changes your clinician must make

Your treatment pressure is prescribed. If aerophagia continues after the self-help steps, ask your clinician to review your therapy data. Depending on your case, they may lower or narrow the pressure range, switch you to auto-PAP, or consider bilevel. Under Medicare, a bilevel device without backup rate is covered for OSA only after CPAP has been tried and proven ineffective ([LCD L33718](https://www.cms.gov/medicare-coverage-database/view/lcd.aspx?LCDId=33718)). Our [Medicare CPAP guide](/us/guides/medicare-cpap-coverage-compliance/) explains the rules.

## Self-check before your appointment

1. **When does it happen?** Every night, or only at higher pressures or in certain positions?
2. **What mask?** Bring the mask, or its name, to the appointment.
3. **Is your nose blocked?** If so, see [nasal congestion](/us/guides/cpap-nasal-congestion/).
4. **Bring your data.** Usage, leak and pressure from the machine or its app.

## When to call your doctor promptly

Stomach pain that is severe, gets worse, or comes with vomiting isn't something to troubleshoot alone. Contact your healthcare provider.

## Verdict

Start with what you control: sleep position and pillows, expiratory pressure relief if it's enabled, and any nasal congestion. Then try a different mask style. If bloating continues, take your therapy data to your clinician and ask about pressure, auto-PAP or bilevel. Don't stop using CPAP while you work it out unless your clinician agrees.
