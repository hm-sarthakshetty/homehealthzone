A travel CPAP is a second machine, light enough to throw in a carry-on, that runs the same auto-CPAP therapy as your bedside unit. In the US the choice comes down to two models: the ResMed AirMini and the Transcend Micro 510.

## Transcend Micro 510: best for most travelers

Transcend's comparison table lists "Universal Mask Compatibility" for the 510, so you can take your usual mask. The 510 also includes a hard-shell travel case, the AirMist waterless humidification adapter, a muffler, and standard and hypoallergenic filters. It is compatible with the PowerAway battery ([Transcend](https://mytranscend.com/product/transcend-micro-510-auto-cpap/)). Its user manual gives a 5-year design life and says the device is for adults weighing over 66 lb (30 kg).

**Trade-offs:** an 8,000 ft maximum altitude, which matters for high mountain towns, and no water humidifier.

## ResMed AirMini: best if you're already on ResMed

The AirMini covers the same 4–20 cmH₂O range. Its catch is masks: ResMed sells AirMini-specific mask kits and setup packs for the P10, N20, N30 and F20 ([ResMed shop](https://eshop.resmed.com/)). cpap.com notes the AirMini version of the P10 "is compatible with the AirMini AutoSet Travel CPAP Machine only" ([cpap.com](https://www.cpap.com/products/airfit-p10-complete-mask-airmini-mask-setup-pack)). If you already use one of those masks at home, a matching AirMini kit keeps the fit you know. ResMed listed the P10 kit at $165 and the F20 kit at $211 on 13 October 2026.

## Flying with a CPAP

- **Screening.** TSA allows CPAPs, BiPAPs and APAPs in carry-on and checked bags. In standard screening, the machine, mask and tubing may stay in the carrying case for X-ray, but you may have to take them out of your carry-on. In PreCheck they can stay in the carry-on. Devices with lithium batteries should be carried in carry-on ([TSA](https://www.tsa.gov/travel/security-screening/whatcanibring/items/nebulizers-cpaps-bipaps-and-apaps)).
- **Carry-on limits.** Under [14 CFR 382.121](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/section-382.121), airlines must let passengers with a disability bring assistive devices into the cabin, and must not count them toward carry-on limits.
- **In-flight use.** Transcend's manual says the 510 meets FAA requirements for in-flight use and asks you to put it in airplane mode (Bluetooth off). Compliance letters are on Transcend's website. Check in-flight use and power with your airline before you fly.
- **Oxygen users.** If you also travel with oxygen, see [FAA rules for portable oxygen concentrators](/us/guides/faa-portable-oxygen-concentrator-rules/).

## Paying for a travel unit

Medicare and most insurers cover one machine. A travel CPAP is usually a cash purchase, and it's a common HSA/FSA purchase ([eligibility guide](/us/guides/hsa-fsa-cpap-oxygen-eligibility/)). Both machines need a prescription before they ship.

## Verdict

Buy the Transcend Micro 510 if you want the lighter, cheaper machine that works with the mask you already own. Buy the AirMini if you already use a ResMed P10, N20, N30 or F20 and want the matching kit. Either way, keep your full-size machine with its heated humidifier for home.
