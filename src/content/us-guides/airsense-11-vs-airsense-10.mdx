## What's the same

The core therapy hasn't changed. Both AutoSet models are prescription-only auto CPAPs for obstructive sleep apnea in patients weighing more than 66 lb. Both offer AutoSet for Her mode for women with mild to moderate OSA. Both have SmartStart, Mask Fit, the same humidity scale, the same 380 mL tub and the same ramp options. Both send therapy data to your care provider wirelessly, with an SD card as the alternative.

## What the AirSense 11 adds

- **Bluetooth and the myAir app.** ResMed's myAir guide says Bluetooth features are only on the AirSense 11. Those include Personal Therapy Assistant (guided setup videos) and **Test Drive**, which introduces pressure gradually (7 and 10 cmH₂O) before your first night ([myAir user guide](https://document.resmed.com/documents/products/data-management/myair/user-guide/myair_user-guide_amer-anz_eng-fre.pdf)). See [myAir and OSCAR explained](/us/guides/myair-vs-oscar-cpap-data/).
- **Higher oxygen limit.** 15 L/min vs 4 L/min. This matters if you need [oxygen bled into your CPAP](/us/guides/cpap-with-oxygen/).
- **Smaller power supply.** 65 W vs a 3.75 A (90 W class) supply, with a lower peak draw (73.2 W vs 104 W). Typical draw is similar.
- **Slightly lighter.** 40 oz vs 44 oz.

## What the AirSense 10 still has going for it

It's the cheaper ResMed listing ($1,008 with heated tubing, vs $1,096 for the AirSense 11 with heated tubing on the same day). Its comfort settings are the same. If you already own one, nothing in ResMed's guides suggests you need to upgrade for therapy reasons. ResMed lists a 5-year design life for the device, so age is a better reason to replace than features.

## Verdict

Buy the AirSense 11 if you're new to CPAP, use high-flow oxygen, or want myAir's Bluetooth features. The AirSense 10 delivers the same pressure and comfort settings for less, so it's a fair choice on a budget. Neither is a reason to give up a working machine early.
