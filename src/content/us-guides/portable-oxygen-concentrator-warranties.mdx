## Warranty vs service life

A long rated service life with a short warranty means you carry the repair risk in later years. The Rove 6 is rated for 8 years with a 3-year warranty; the OxyGo NEXT is rated for 5 years with a 5-year warranty. Inogen's site attaches a condition to its 8 years ("when used less than 5 hours per day"), which its manuals don't.

## Before you buy

- Ask whether the price includes the standard or extended warranty.
- Confirm who handles repairs: the maker, the retailer or your oxygen supplier.
- For a used unit, assume no maker warranty unless the statement says otherwise ([used concentrators](/us/guides/used-refurbished-oxygen-concentrator/)).
- Budget for batteries: they're rated for hundreds of cycles, not the device's full life ([POC battery life](/us/guides/portable-oxygen-concentrator-battery-life/)).
- If Medicare pays, you rent through a supplier rather than buying, and the supplier's obligations apply instead ([Medicare oxygen coverage](/us/guides/medicare-oxygen-coverage/)).

## Verdict

If warranty length is your priority, the OxyGo NEXT and Oxlife Independence lead at 5 years. CAIRE's 3 years includes sieve beds. Inogen's standard 3 years covers sieve beds for only 1 year, but its 8-year rated life is the longest, and a lifetime option is available.
