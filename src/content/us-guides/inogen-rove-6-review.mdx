## Source conflicts we found

- **Sound:** the manual and product page say 39 dBA at setting 2; Inogen's March 2026 [spec sheet](https://www.inogen.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Inogen-Rove-6-Spec-Sheet.pdf) says 37 dBA.
- **Service life conditions:** the manual states 8 years with no condition. The consumer FAQ says "up to 8 years when used less than 5 hours per day"; Inogen's provider site says "less than 8 hours per day".
- **Page copy:** the product page's comparison block shows "Up to 6 hrs 7 mins" of battery, which matches the Rove 4, not the Rove 6. We use the manual's table.

## Strengths and limits

| Strengths | Limits |
| --- | --- |
| Six settings up to 1,260 mL/min | Pulse only: no continuous flow |
| 8-year rated service life | Not for use with CPAP, per the manual |
| Long battery at low settings | Short runtime at settings 5–6 on the standard battery |
| Same output as the G5 it replaces | 3-year standard warranty is shorter than OxyGo's 5 |

## Verdict

From the documents, the Rove 6 is a well-specified mid-weight pulse POC with a wide setting range and Inogen's longest rated service life. If you're on setting 5 or 6, budget for the extended battery. The [OxyGo NEXT](/us/guides/oxygo-next-review/) matches its output with a longer warranty, and the [CAIRE FreeStyle Comfort](/us/guides/caire-freestyle-comfort-review/) is the main rival.
