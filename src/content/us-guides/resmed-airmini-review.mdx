## What stands out on paper

- **Size and weight.** At 10.6 oz it's very small, though Transcend's Micro 510 manual lists an even lighter 0.49 lb (about 7.9 oz). Compare others in [best travel CPAP machines](/us/guides/best-travel-cpap-machines/).
- **Full auto algorithms.** It runs ResMed's AutoSet and AutoSet for Her, the same mode names as the [AirSense 11](/us/guides/airsense-11-settings/).
- **Low power draw.** 6.3 W typical makes battery planning easier ([CPAP battery power](/us/guides/cpap-battery-camping-power/)). The user guide doesn't describe an internal battery.
- **Waterless humidity.** HumidX cartridges replace a water tub. ResMed says HumidX Plus adds humidity in dry conditions, at altitude or on a plane, and both must be replaced within 30 days of opening.

## Flying with it

ResMed says the AirMini meets FAA requirements for all phases of air travel, and compliance letters can be downloaded from ResMed.com. On board: enter airplane mode by holding the Bluetooth button for at least ten seconds (light turns white), and don't use the app. Airlines' carry-on policies vary.

## Who it's for

## Verdict

On paper, the AirMini is a serious travel machine: ResMed's AutoSet algorithms, 4–20 cmH2O, 10.6 oz and FAA compliance. The costs are the proprietary tubing, a short mask list and waterless-only humidity. Its $919 price is above the [Transcend Micro 510](/us/guides/transcend-micro-510-review/) (<Price slug="transcend-micro-510" country="US" collection="us-products" />). Buy it as a second, travel machine if you already use a compatible ResMed mask.
