A cruise is one of the harder trips for oxygen users: you're away from your supplier for days, often outside the U.S., and every cruise line has its own rules. Here's what's verifiable from primary sources, and what you need to confirm yourself.

## Rule 1: you bring the oxygen

Royal Caribbean's FAQ is explicit: "If you are dependent on oxygen or require oxygen therapy, you must supply your own oxygen, and meet certain requirements prior to boarding the ship." It directs guests to its Access Department at 866-592-7225 ([Royal Caribbean](https://www.royalcaribbean.com/faq/questions/onboard-oxygen-tank-policy)). Assume your line is similar until it tells you otherwise in writing.

## Rule 2: Medicare may not follow you

Medicare's oxygen policy article says "Medicare does not cover items or services provided/used outside the United States and its territories. The supplier is not required to provide or arrange for oxygen use in those situations" ([CMS A52514](https://www.cms.gov/medicare-coverage-database/view/article.aspx?articleId=52514)). If your itinerary leaves U.S. territory, ask your supplier exactly what it will and won't provide for your itinerary. For trips within the U.S., the supplier's travel duties are covered in our [altitude guide](/us/guides/oxygen-at-altitude-denver/) and [supplier switching guide](/us/guides/switch-medicare-oxygen-supplier/).

## Your options for oxygen on board

| Option | Pros | Watch-outs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Your own POC | Familiar, light, works ashore | Battery capacity on excursions; pulse-dose may not suit sleep |
| Rented POC | No purchase; can match the trip | Book early; check delivery to port or ship and return terms ([renting a POC](/us/guides/rent-portable-oxygen-concentrator-travel/)) |
| Stationary concentrator in the cabin | Continuous flow for sleep | Heavy; needs cabin power; ask the line about delivery and storage |
| Cylinders / liquid | No power needed | Storage limits on board; ask the line whether they're allowed and how much |

Specific cruise line limits on cylinders or liquid oxygen weren't verifiable from primary sources for this guide, so ask your line directly.

## Power and batteries

| Planning item | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Battery runtime | Use the manufacturer's tables, not marketing figures ([POC battery life](/us/guides/portable-oxygen-concentrator-battery-life/)) |
| Shore days | Bring enough charged batteries for the whole excursion plus a margin |
| Cabin charging | Use only the manufacturer's power supplies (Inogen Rove 6 manual warns against non-specified ones, [manual](https://www.inogen.com/rove6-user-manuals/pdf/96-13595-00-01%20A%20Inogen%20Rove%206%20US%20User%20Manual_EN_ES_February%202026.pdf)) |
| Sleep | If you need oxygen at night, check that your device is suitable ([POCs for sleeping](/us/guides/portable-oxygen-concentrator-for-sleeping/)) |

## Shore excursions

Royal Caribbean asks guests taking an oxygen cylinder or concentrator on tour to provide the device's dimensions (length × width × height) in advance on its Accessible Shore Excursion form, sent to shorexaccess@rccl.com ([Royal Caribbean](https://www.royalcaribbean.com/faq/questions/onboard-oxygen-tank-policy)).

## Flying to the port

If you fly to embarkation, FAA rules apply to your POC and batteries. See [FAA POC rules](/us/guides/faa-portable-oxygen-concentrator-rules/). Medicare policy says airline-furnished oxygen is non-covered and is the passenger's cost ([CMS A52514](https://www.cms.gov/medicare-coverage-database/view/article.aspx?articleId=52514)).

## Booking checklist

| When | Task |
| --- | --- |
| Before booking | Ask the line for its oxygen policy and required forms in writing |
| Before booking | Ask your clinician whether you're fit to travel and for settings at rest, on exertion and asleep |
| At booking | Notify the line's access/special-needs team; give device type and quantity |
| 4–8 weeks out | Arrange rental or supply; confirm delivery to port or ship |
| 4–8 weeks out | Ask your Medicare supplier what it covers for the trip |
| Before sailing | Pack prescription copy, manuals, chargers, spare cannulas, enough batteries |
| On board | Follow [fire safety rules](/us/guides/home-oxygen-fire-safety/): no smoking or flames near oxygen |

## Verdict

Treat a cruise as a self-supplied trip. Confirm your cruise line's oxygen rules in writing, plan to rent or bring a POC with plenty of batteries, and don't rely on Medicare once you leave U.S. territory. Royal Caribbean's published policy is a useful model of what to expect: bring your own and tell the Access Department early.
