## Before the season

| Step | Source |
| --- | --- |
| Create a Smart911 profile and list power-dependent medical equipment in the medical section | NOLA Ready |
| Check whether you qualify for City-Assisted Evacuation pickup | NOLA Ready |
| Know your evacuation zone and routes | Ready.gov |
| Keep phones charged and buy backup chargers | Ready.gov |
| Pack medication and supplies; access may be cut for days or weeks | Ready.gov |
| Agree an outage and evacuation plan with your clinician and supplier | Ready.gov ([our outage plan](/us/guides/power-outage-plan-cpap-oxygen/)) |

The manuals specify non-condensing humidity, so keep devices dry and indoors in air conditioning where possible.

## After the storm

- Don't touch electrical equipment if it's wet or you're standing in water (Ready.gov).
- People with lung conditions shouldn't enter buildings with water leaks or visible or smelled mold (Ready.gov).
- Run generators outdoors only ([power outage plan](/us/guides/power-outage-plan-cpap-oxygen/)).

## Verdict

For CPAP and oxygen users in New Orleans, the main risk is losing power or having to evacuate. Register, pack early and agree a backup plan with your supplier.
