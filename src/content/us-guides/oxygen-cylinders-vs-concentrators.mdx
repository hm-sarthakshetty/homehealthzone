U.S. home oxygen comes in four basic forms, and many patients use two: one at home, one for getting out. Here's how they compare, using CMS Medicare policy, the American Thoracic Society (ATS) guideline and VA patient-safety data.

## The four modalities

| Modality | How it works | Needs power? | Refills? | Typical role |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Stationary concentrator | Makes oxygen from room air | Yes | No | Main home source; the VA says concentrators are the most common source |
| Gaseous cylinders | Compressed oxygen in tanks | No | Yes, delivered or home-filled | Portable use, backup in outages |
| Liquid oxygen | Liquid oxygen in a home reservoir; you fill portable units | No | Yes, reservoir deliveries | Higher-flow mobility (ATS) |
| Portable concentrator (POC) | Battery concentrator; CMS requires at least 85% concentration | Battery/AC/DC | No | Mobility and travel |
| Transfill (home fill) | Stationary concentrator feature that fills your own portable cylinders | Yes | Self-filled | Mobility without deliveries |

Sources: [CMS A52514](https://www.cms.gov/medicare-coverage-database/view/article.aspx?articleId=52514), [VA AL18-01](https://www.patientsafety.va.gov/docs/alerts/INTERNET_AL18-01.pdf), [ATS 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7667898/).

## What the ATS guideline adds

The ATS 2020 home oxygen guideline suggests **portable liquid oxygen** for mobile patients who need **continuous flow above 3 L/min during exertion** (conditional recommendation). It also recommends that patients and caregivers get education on equipment and safety ([ATS](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7667898/)). Many POCs are pulse-dose only, so check that one meets your exertional prescription: see [pulse dose vs continuous flow](/us/guides/pulse-dose-vs-continuous-flow/).

## Medicare rules that shape your choice

| Rule | What CMS says | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Contents included | Payment for stationary and portable oxygen contents is included in the stationary equipment allowance during rental | [A52514](https://www.cms.gov/medicare-coverage-database/view/article.aspx?articleId=52514) |
| After month 36 | If you used gaseous or liquid equipment in the 36th month, contents payments can continue | A52514 |
| POC definition (E1392) | Portable, 85% or greater oxygen, runs on AC or DC, batteries giving at least 2 hours at 2 LPM equivalency, total weight with those batteries 20 lb or less | A52514 |
| Transfill | K0738 (fills gaseous cylinders) or E0433 (fills liquid); when billed, the separate portable gaseous (E0431) or liquid (E0434) system code isn't used | A52514 |
| Changing modality | Not allowed unless: replaced with the same or equivalent item; your treating practitioner orders different equipment; you choose an upgrade and sign an ABN; or CMS/DME MAC decides it's warranted | A52514 |
| Concentrator maintenance | After 36 months, concentrator or transfill users may owe coinsurance for maintenance every 6 months, when the supplier visits | [Medicare.gov](https://www.medicare.gov/coverage/oxygen-equipment-accessories) |

So if you want a POC instead of cylinders, the practical route is a new order from your clinician. See [Medicare home oxygen coverage](/us/guides/medicare-oxygen-coverage/).

## Head-to-head

| Factor | Concentrator (stationary) | Cylinders | Liquid | POC |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Works in a power outage | No | Yes | Yes | Battery only |
| Delivery dependence | None | Yes (unless transfill) | Yes | None |
| Electricity cost | Yes ([cost guide](/us/guides/oxygen-concentrator-electricity-cost/)) | No | No | Charging |
| Fire risk | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Night use | Continuous flow | Ask your supplier about duration | Ask your supplier about duration | Depends on model ([POCs for sleeping](/us/guides/portable-oxygen-concentrator-for-sleeping/)) |

The VA notes that concentrators, cylinders and liquid reservoirs "all three sources pose a risk of accelerated combustion" ([VA](https://www.patientsafety.va.gov/docs/alerts/INTERNET_AL18-01.pdf)).

## Storing cylinders safely

MedlinePlus says don't store oxygen in a trunk, box or small closet; under the bed is OK if air moves freely; and keep oil-, grease- or alcohol-based cleaners away ([MedlinePlus](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/patientinstructions/000049.htm)). Follow your supplier's instructions for securing cylinders. More in [home oxygen fire safety](/us/guides/home-oxygen-fire-safety/).

## What we left out

How long a cylinder lasts depends on its size, fill and your flow. We didn't find a primary U.S. source with a duration table we could cite, so we don't publish one. Ask your supplier for the duration chart for the exact cylinders and regulator or conserver they provide.

## Verdict

For most people a stationary concentrator at home plus a portable option is the standard set-up. Choose cylinders or transfill if you want a backup that works without power, liquid if you need high continuous flow on the move (per ATS), and a POC if its delivery matches your prescription. Under Medicare, changing type needs a practitioner's order or another allowed reason, so start with your clinician.
