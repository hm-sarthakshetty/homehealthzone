A blocked or runny nose is a common reason people struggle in their first weeks of CPAP. If your nose is blocked, you may end up breathing through your mouth, which brings its own problems: [dry mouth](/us/guides/cpap-dry-mouth/) and, on a nasal mask, mouth leak.

## Step 1: humidity and heated tubing

On the ResMed AirSense 11, the user guide links a dry or blocked nose to a humidity level that's set too low: "Increase the Humidity Level." Humidity runs from Off and 1 (lowest) to 8 (highest). With ClimateLineAir 11 heated tubing and the HumidAir 11 tub, **Climate Control Auto** aims for a constant 85% relative humidity, which ResMed says is designed "to prevent dryness in the nose and mouth" ([ResMed AirSense 11 user guide](https://document.resmed.com/documents/products/machine/airsense-11/user-guide/airsense11_user-guide_amer_mul.pdf)).

If more humidity leaves water droplets in your mask or tube, see [CPAP rainout](/us/guides/cpap-rainout/).

## Step 2: nasal care

- **Saline spray** before bed ([MedlinePlus](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/001916.htm)).
- **Non-petroleum cream** in the nostrils before using PAP ([AASM PAP troubleshooting FAQ](https://sleepeducation.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/PAP-FAQs.pdf)).
- **Prescription nasal sprays**: the AASM and ATS both say to discuss these with your healthcare provider.

## Step 3: check your mask

Leaks can dry the nose. The ResMed troubleshooting guide's checks for dryness start with "Are there any leaks?" and the humidity setting ([ResMed troubleshooting guide](https://document.resmed.com/documents/products/serviceandsupport/air-solutions-troubleshooting-guide/Air%20Solutions%20Troubleshooting%20Guide%20AMER%20Eng.pdf)). See [mask leaks](/us/guides/cpap-mask-leak/).

If your nose stays blocked, the AASM lists a **full face mask** covering the nose and mouth as an option. Ask your supplier to compare options ([mask guide](/us/guides/best-cpap-masks/)).

## Step 4: when to see a specialist

If congestion persists despite these steps, the AASM suggests consultation with an **ear, nose and throat (ENT) specialist**. MedlinePlus also lists nosebleeds among the problems some people have when starting PAP. Tell your provider if you get them.

## Verdict

Turn the humidity up first, and use heated tubing if your machine supports it. Add saline spray or a non-petroleum nasal cream, check for leaks, and ask your provider about prescription sprays if it doesn't settle. If your nose stays blocked, a full face mask and an ENT review are the next steps.
