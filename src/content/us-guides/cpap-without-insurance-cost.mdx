## Step 1: diagnosis

You can't buy a CPAP in the US without a prescription. cpap.com and ResMed's shop both state that their machines and masks require one before shipment. That means a sleep test. cpap.com listed its home sleep test at $99, and labels it HSA/FSA eligible. Whether a home test is right for you is a clinical question. If you later want Medicare coverage, its sleep-test and evaluation rules are strict ([Medicare CPAP guide](/us/guides/medicare-cpap-coverage-compliance/)).

## Step 3: the mask

ResMed's US shop listed complete masks from $105 (AirFit P10, AirFit N20) to $167 (AirFit F30i). Budget for a second try if the first mask doesn't suit you, and check the seller's exchange terms first. cpap.com, for example, advertises a 60-night mask guarantee. See [best CPAP masks](/us/guides/best-cpap-masks/).

## Step 4: supplies, every year

Using ResMed shop prices for an AirSense 11 with an AirFit F20 mask, a full year of replacements at Medicare's usual maximum rates comes to **$1,368**. Replacing the mask and heated tubing every 6 months instead comes to **$1,068**. The full breakdown is in [CPAP supplies replacement schedule](/us/guides/cpap-supplies-replacement-schedule/). Nasal pillow users pay less: P10 replacement pillows are $22, against $54 for an F20 cushion.

## Ways to cut the cost

1. **Use pre-tax money.** IRS Pub 502 defines medical expenses to include equipment, supplies and diagnostic devices for treating disease, so HSA or FSA funds usually apply ([HSA/FSA guide](/us/guides/hsa-fsa-cpap-oxygen-eligibility/)).
2. **Pick the lower-cost machine.** The React Health G3 X was $131 cheaper than the AirSense 11 at the same retailer on the same day.
3. **Replace on wear, not on the calendar.** Medicare's schedule is the most it will pay for, not a manufacturer requirement. Replace cushions and filters first.
4. **Use subscriptions and sales.** cpap.com advertises saving 10% on scheduled supply deliveries and was running a sitewide sale on 13 October 2026. Recheck prices before you buy.
5. **Check whether you're eligible for coverage.** If you have Medicare, renting through a supplier costs you 20% of the approved amount, and you own the machine after 13 months ([medicare.gov](https://www.medicare.gov/coverage/continuous-positive-airway-pressure-devices)).

## Verdict

Expect to spend about $1,100–$1,300 to start CPAP without insurance, then a few hundred to about $1,400 a year on supplies, depending on how often you replace parts. The cheapest sensible route is a home sleep test if appropriate, the React Health G3 X, a nasal or pillow mask if you breathe through your nose, HSA or FSA money, and replacing parts when they wear out.
