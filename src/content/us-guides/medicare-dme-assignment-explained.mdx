## Three kinds of supplier

| Supplier type | What you pay | Claims |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Participating (always accepts assignment) | Part B deductible + 20% of the approved amount | Supplier files; can't charge for filing |
| Non-participating, accepts assignment for you | Same as above, case by case | Supplier files |
| Non-participating, doesn't accept assignment | May pay in full up front; can be charged more | Supplier should still file; you can file yourself if it refuses |
| Opted out of Medicare | Medicare won't pay (except emergencies); private contract | None |

Medicare.gov says providers who opt out do so for at least 2 years.

## Why it matters for DME

Medicare.gov's general 15% "limiting charge" describes non-participating health care providers. For DME, CMS says non-participating suppliers in non-bidding areas aren't required to accept assignment and "can charge any amount." Medicare also pays only for DME from suppliers enrolled in Medicare ([Medicare.gov](https://www.medicare.gov/coverage/durable-medical-equipment-dme-coverage)).

## Rentals: ask about every month

CPAP and oxygen are usually rented. Medicare.gov advises making sure the supplier accepts assignment for **all rental months**; otherwise you may have to pay the full cost up front and wait for Medicare to reimburse its share.

## Questions to ask before you sign

1. Are you enrolled in Medicare?
2. Do you participate in Medicare, or accept assignment for my claims?
3. Will you accept assignment for every rental month and every resupply order?
4. Which brands and models do you supply? (Contract suppliers must offer the same range to Medicare and non-Medicare customers, per CMS.)
5. Who handles repairs and replacements?

## Verdict

Assignment is the simplest protection against surprise DME bills. Ask the five questions above, get the answers in writing, and pair an assignment supplier with Medigap if you want your 20% covered too ([Medigap and DME](/us/guides/medigap-cpap-oxygen-costs/)).
