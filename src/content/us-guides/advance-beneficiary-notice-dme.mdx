## The 3 options

| Option | You get the item? | Claim sent to Medicare? | Can you appeal? | If Medicare pays |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Yes | Yes | Yes | Supplier refunds you, minus copays or deductibles* |
| 2 | Yes | No | No | Not applicable |
| 3 | No | No | No | Not applicable |

*CMS lets suppliers that don't accept assignment strike the refund sentence and add a statement that you're responsible for their charge.

## When you might see one for CPAP or oxygen

The ABN lists the reason the supplier expects Medicare won't pay. For CPAP and oxygen, check the reason against Medicare's rules: for example, supplies more often than the usual limits ([CPAP supplies schedule](/us/guides/cpap-supplies-replacement-schedule/)), or coverage criteria not documented ([Medicare CPAP rules](/us/guides/medicare-cpap-coverage-compliance/), [Medicare oxygen coverage](/us/guides/medicare-oxygen-coverage/)).

## What to do

1. Read the reason and the estimated cost.
2. Ask your doctor whether documentation could fix the problem.
3. If you want the item and the right to appeal, choose Option 1.
4. Keep a copy, then watch your [Medicare Summary Notice](/us/guides/medicare-summary-notice-dme/) and [appeal](/us/guides/medicare-dme-appeal/) if needed.

## Verdict

An ABN is a cost warning, not a final decision. Option 1 keeps your options open.
