General information, not legal advice. For Original Medicare, see [appealing a Medicare DME denial](/us/guides/medicare-dme-appeal/).

## The first two levels

| Step | What happens | Deadline / timeline |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Organization determination | You, your representative or your provider asks the plan whether an item is covered | Ask the plan |
| Level 1: plan reconsideration | You ask the plan to reconsider a denial | File within 65 days of the denial notice |
| Plan decision | Standard pre-service 30 days; payment 60 days; fast 72 hours | Up to 14-day extension in some cases |
| Level 2: Independent Review Entity | Automatic if the plan denies again | Same 30 / 60 days / 72 hours |
| Level 3: OMHA (ALJ) | You ask within 60 days of the IRE decision | $200 minimum in 2026 |

## What to include

- Your name, address and Medicare Number
- The items (for example a CPAP, mask resupply or oxygen concentrator), dates and reasons you're appealing
- Your representative's details and proof, if you have one
- Supporting information, such as a doctor's note

Medicare.gov suggests asking your supplier for information that could strengthen your appeal. If your appeal is for something you haven't received yet, your doctor can ask for the reconsideration for you.

## Check the denial against Medicare's rules

Under CMS rules, Medicare Advantage plans must follow Original Medicare's coverage criteria. If the denial cites a requirement that isn't in Medicare's CPAP or oxygen rules, say so in your appeal ([MA prior authorization rules](/us/guides/medicare-advantage-prior-authorization-cpap/)).

## Verdict

File within 65 days, ask for a fast appeal if waiting would harm your health, and remember that a second denial goes to independent review automatically.
