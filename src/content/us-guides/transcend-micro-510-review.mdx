## The "auto" question

Transcend's product page calls the Micro 510 an "Auto CPAP" with "auto-adjusting pressure". The performance table in the Micro 510 manual we downloaded from that page lists "Ventilator Mode: CPAP", with a note that Ramp and AirRelief "do not represent separate ventilation modes". We can't resolve that from the documents. If you need auto-adjusting pressure ([APAP vs CPAP](/us/guides/apap-vs-cpap-vs-bipap/)), ask Transcend or your prescriber to confirm before buying.

## Strengths on paper

- **Lightest we've documented:** 0.49 lb, lighter than the [AirMini](/us/guides/resmed-airmini-review/) (10.6 oz).
- **Battery option:** the PowerAway battery attaches directly ([CPAP battery power](/us/guides/cpap-battery-camping-power/)).
- **Longer warranty than the AirMini:** 3 years vs ResMed's 2.
- **Data:** the MySleepDash app for therapy data.

## Flying

The manual says it meets FAA requirements (RTCA/DO-160 section 21, category M). Check your airline's carry-on and in-flight power policy before you fly.

## Verdict

From the documents, the Micro 510 is the lightest and cheapest of the two main travel CPAPs we track, with a battery option and a 3-year warranty. The mode question is the key issue: if your prescription is for auto-adjusting pressure, get written confirmation that this unit provides it. For a confirmed auto machine, compare the [ResMed AirMini](/us/guides/resmed-airmini-review/).
