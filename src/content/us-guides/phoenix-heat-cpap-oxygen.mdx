## Phoenix temperatures (Sky Harbor, 1991–2020)

| Month | Normal high | Normal low |
| --- | --- | --- |
| May | 94.5°F | 69.5°F |
| June | 104.2°F | 78.6°F |
| July | 106.5°F | 84.5°F |
| August | 105.1°F | 83.6°F |
| September | 100.4°F | 78.1°F |
| October | 89.2°F | 65.6°F |

NWS Phoenix (1991–2020): an average of 111 days a year at or above 100°F and 21 days at or above 110°F. Sky Harbor's station elevation is 337.4 m (about 1,107 ft), so altitude isn't a concern here.

## Practical steps

| Situation | What the documents support |
| --- | --- |
| Using CPAP at night | Indoors in air conditioning; Phoenix's normal summer lows (78–85°F) are within the 95°F limit |
| Using a POC outdoors in summer | Above 104°F you're outside the manuals' operating range; plan shade, air-conditioned stops and your clinician's advice |
| Leaving devices in a car | The Rove 6 and AirSense 11 manuals list storage to 158°F, but operation only to 104°F and 95°F; let the device cool before use |
| Rainout from cold air conditioning | Heated tubing and Climate Control ([ClimateLineAir explained](/us/guides/climatelineair-heated-tubing/), [CPAP rainout](/us/guides/cpap-rainout/)) |
| Summer outages | Ready.gov advises a plan for power-dependent medical devices ([power outage plan](/us/guides/power-outage-plan-cpap-oxygen/)) |

## Verdict

Phoenix heat is mainly a problem for equipment used outdoors or in vehicles. Keep CPAP and home concentrators in air-conditioned rooms, treat 104°F as the outer limit for most portable concentrators and 95°F for CPAP machines, and have an outage plan for the summer.
