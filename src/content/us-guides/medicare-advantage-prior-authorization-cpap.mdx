faqs:
  - q: "Does Original Medicare require prior authorization for CPAP or oxygen?"
    a: "Not on CMS's Required Prior Authorization List (updated 29 July 2026), which covers items such as power mobility devices, some orthoses and lower limb prosthetics. CPAP and oxygen codes aren't on it."
  - q: "Can a Medicare Advantage plan require prior authorization for CPAP?"
    a: "Yes, plans may. Under CMS's 2024 rule (CMS-4201-F), MA plans must follow Traditional Medicare's NCDs and LCDs, and coordinated care plans may use prior authorization only to confirm diagnoses or other medical criteria, or medical necessity."
  - q: "How fast must a plan decide?"
    a: "CMS-0057-F requires Medicare Advantage organizations and other impacted payers to decide within 72 hours for expedited requests and 7 calendar days for standard requests, generally beginning January 1, 2026."
  - q: "What if I switch MA plans during treatment?"
    a: "CMS-4201-F requires coordinated care plans to give at least a 90-day transition period in which the new plan can't require prior authorization for an active course of treatment."
  - q: "Will I be told why I was denied?"
    a: "Beginning in 2026, CMS-0057-F requires impacted payers to give a specific reason for denied prior authorization decisions."

Your plan's Evidence of Coverage has its own list.

## Original Medicare vs Medicare Advantage

| Topic | Original Medicare | Medicare Advantage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Prior authorization for CPAP or oxygen | Not on CMS's Required Prior Authorization List | Plan may require it |
| Coverage criteria | NCDs and LCDs | Must follow NCDs, LCDs and Traditional Medicare rules (CMS-4201-F) |
| Purpose of prior authorization | Not applicable | Coordinated care plans: only to confirm diagnosis or medical criteria, or medical necessity |
| Switching plans mid-treatment | Not applicable | At least 90 days without new prior authorization for an active course of treatment |
| Decision time | Not applicable | 72 hours expedited, 7 calendar days standard (CMS-0057-F, generally from 1 Jan 2026) |
| Denial reason | Not applicable | Specific reason required from 2026 |
| Approval length | Not applicable | Valid as long as medically reasonable and necessary for the course of treatment |

## What this means for CPAP users

Because MA plans must follow Medicare's CPAP rules, prior authorization shouldn't add criteria beyond the LCD ([Medicare CPAP rules](/us/guides/medicare-cpap-coverage-compliance/)). If you're denied, ask for the specific reason, check it against the LCD, and use your plan's appeal process.

## Verdict

Prior authorization is mainly a Medicare Advantage issue for CPAP and oxygen. Know the 2026 timeframes, ask for the denial reason in writing, and compare it with Medicare's own criteria.
