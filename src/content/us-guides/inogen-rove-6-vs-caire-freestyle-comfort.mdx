## Output and dosing: the real difference

The Rove 6 delivers a set volume per minute, divided across your breaths. At 15 breaths a minute, setting 2 gives 28 mL per breath; at 30 breaths a minute, 14 mL.

The FreeStyle Comfort's **autoSAT** (on by default) keeps the dose per breath fixed as your breathing rate rises, up to 40 breaths a minute. So total oxygen per minute increases when you're active. At setting 2, autoSAT gives 21 mL per breath at every breath rate CAIRE lists, 15 to 40 a minute ([FreeStyle Comfort manual](https://files.caireinc.com/Manuals/MN232-0_B.pdf)). You can switch autoSAT off for traditional per-minute dosing.

Which suits you depends on how your saturation holds during activity. Your clinician should test you on the specific device ([pulse vs continuous](/us/guides/pulse-dose-vs-continuous-flow/)).

## Battery

The Rove 6 lists longer runtimes at setting 1 and 2 with either battery. At setting 5, the FreeStyle Comfort single battery (2:00) edges the Rove 6 standard battery (1:45). The full per-setting table is in [battery life by model](/us/guides/portable-oxygen-concentrator-battery-life/). The Rove 6's batteries are rated for 500 cycles and the FreeStyle Comfort's for 300, which matters over years of daily use.

## Living with it

- **Shape:** the FreeStyle Comfort is flatter (3.1 in deep) and CAIRE says it's light enough to carry on a shoulder strap. The Rove 6 is taller and narrower (7.18 × 3.27 × 8.14 in).
- **Noise:** Inogen lists 39 dBA typical at setting 2. CAIRE's newest manual lists a 43.0 dBA maximum at setting 2. The figures aren't measured the same way.
- **Apps:** myCAIRE sends usage hours and flow settings to your home care provider. Inogen Connect handles data log transfers and software updates over Bluetooth.
- **Not with CPAP:** the Rove 6 manual says not to use it with CPAP, humidifiers or nebulizers.

## Verdict

The Rove 6 is the more capable all-rounder: more output, longer listed battery life, longer rated service life and longer-lived batteries. The FreeStyle Comfort costs less in our check and its autoSAT dosing is a genuine difference for active users, so it's worth trialling if your clinician agrees. Both fly.
