In the US, a CPAP purchase starts with a sleep study and a prescription. US retailers will not ship a CPAP without one. [cpap.com's AirSense 11 listing](https://www.cpap.com/products/resmed-airsense-11-autoset) states that it "requires a prescription before shipment". Once you have it, the decision is mostly about how you pay: through a Medicare or insurance supplier, or in cash.

## Medicare: a 12-week trial, then a 13-month rental

[Medicare Part B may cover CPAP therapy](https://www.medicare.gov/coverage/continuous-positive-airway-pressure-devices) after an obstructive sleep apnea diagnosis:

- Coverage starts with a 12-week trial of the device and accessories.
- To keep coverage, you meet your provider in person and they document that you meet the conditions and the therapy is helping. CMS guidance for suppliers ties this to [documented adherence and symptom improvement](https://www.cms.gov/training-education/medicare-learning-networkr-mln/compliance/medicare-provider-compliance-tips/cpap-devices-accessories).
- Medicare pays the supplier to rent the machine for 13 continuous months. After that, you own it.
- You pay 20% of the Medicare-approved amount after the Part B deductible, if the supplier accepts assignment.

Use the machine every night from day one. Your adherence data, which the machine records, is what keeps coverage going after the trial.

### ResMed AirSense 11 AutoSet

The AirSense 11 is the primary home machine. It runs CPAP, AutoSet and AutoSet for Her modes across 4–20 cmH₂O, with a built-in heated humidifier, climate control, and support for a heated tube, per published spec. The US retail SKU is the AirSense 11 AutoSet with HumidAir.

### ResMed AirMini AutoSet

The AirMini weighs 0.3 kg, which makes it the travel unit. It covers the same 4–20 cmH₂O range but has no water humidifier, only waterless humidification. Buy it as a second machine for flights and trips, not as your only CPAP.

## Philips in the US

After the June 2021 foam recall, a [2024 federal consent decree](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/federal-court-enters-consent-decree-against-philips-respironics-following-recall-certain-sleep) restricts Philips Respironics, with limited exceptions, from making and selling new CPAP and BiPAP machines from several US facilities until it meets FDA requirements. Owners of recalled devices should follow the remediation steps on the FDA page.

## Verdict

Buy the ResMed AirSense 11 AutoSet as the home machine, through your Medicare or insurance supplier if you are covered, or for cash at around $1,000 if you are not. Add the AirMini only if you travel often enough to justify a second device. Bring the prescription and sleep study to whichever channel you use, and settle the pressure settings with your sleep physician.
