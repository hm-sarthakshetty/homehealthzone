A stationary concentrator runs a compressor for most of the day, so it shows up on your power bill. Here's what it costs, using manufacturer power figures and the latest U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA) residential rates.

## The formula

**Monthly cost = watts ÷ 1,000 × hours per day × 30 days × your rate per kWh.**

Find your rate on your bill: divide total energy charges by kWh used. The EIA's July 2026 U.S. average residential price was **18.31¢/kWh**, up from 17.45¢ in July 2025 ([EIA Electric Power Monthly, Table 5.6.A](https://www.eia.gov/electricity/monthly/epm_table_grapher.php?t=epmt_5_6_a), released September 2026).

Manufacturer figures are rated or average power, so actual draw varies with setting and conditions. Lower settings generally draw less: CAIRE says the Companion 5 uses "less than 300 W at 3.0 LPM and lower" ([CAIRE](https://www.caireinc.com/product/companion-5/)).

## Ways to keep the cost down

- **Use the prescribed setting, not more.** Higher flow uses more power on most units, and the FDA warns against self-adjusting oxygen ([FDA](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USFDA/bulletins/2a8d8e8)).
- **Pick the right size.** If your prescription fits a 5 LPM unit, a 10 LPM machine costs more to run.
- **Keep filters clean.** CAIRE lists weekly intake-filter cleaning for the Intensity 10 ([cut sheet](https://files.caireinc.com/CutSheets-Lit/ML-CONC0084.pdf)).
- **Ask your utility** about medical-need programs. These vary and HHZ hasn't verified any specific one.

## Power outages

A concentrator stops when the power does. Ask your supplier about backup cylinders. For battery and generator planning, see [CPAP battery and camping power](/us/guides/cpap-battery-camping-power/) and [POC battery life](/us/guides/portable-oxygen-concentrator-battery-life/).

## Does Medicare cover the electricity?

Medicare.gov says the oxygen rental covers equipment, refills, supplies and maintenance ([Medicare.gov](https://www.medicare.gov/coverage/oxygen-equipment-accessories)). It doesn't list electricity, and we found no primary source saying Medicare reimburses it. See [Medicare home oxygen coverage](/us/guides/medicare-oxygen-coverage/).

## Verdict

Budget roughly $35–$85 a month for 24/7 use at the U.S. average rate. The low end is a 5 LPM unit at low flow, the high end a 10 LPM unit. At July 2026 rates, Massachusetts, New York and California cost about 1.6–1.8 times the national average, and Hawaii about 2.6 times. Calculate with your own rate and setting, and choose the smallest machine that meets your prescription.
