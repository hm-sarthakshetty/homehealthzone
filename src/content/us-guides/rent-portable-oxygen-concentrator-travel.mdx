If you use oxygen at home but your equipment isn't practical for a flight or vacation, a rented portable oxygen concentrator (POC) is the usual answer. Here are real rates and terms, and what you need to sort out.

## What it costs (example: OxygenToGo, 2026)

| Item | Rate |
| --- | --- |
| First week | $399 |
| Each additional week | $299 |
| Continuous 24/7 flow device | +$100 per week |
| Shipping (to and from) | Extra, billed to you |
| Optional rental insurance | $125 per week, $500 deductible |
| Late return (overtime) | $100 per day |
| Shortest term | 1 week (weeks rounded up) |
| Batteries | 3 included; extras on request |
| Monthly | Quote on request |

Source: [OxygenToGo POC rental forms 2026](https://oxygentogo.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/POC-RENTAL-FORMS-2026-V2.pdf), read 13 October 2026. The form lists a stipulated equipment value of $3,500 if the equipment isn't returned. Prices vary by company. Get a written quote including shipping.

## Before you book

| Step | Detail | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Get a prescription | Required before the rental ships | OxygenToGo, FDA |
| Ask about pulse vs continuous | Your clinician should confirm a pulse setting works for you; continuous costs more | [Pulse vs continuous](/us/guides/pulse-dose-vs-continuous-flow/) |
| Check insurance | OxygenToGo doesn't bill Medicare, Medicaid or private insurance | OxygenToGo |
| Ask your Medicare supplier first | If you're on Medicare oxygen, your supplier provides your equipment and service ([Medicare oxygen](/us/guides/medicare-oxygen-coverage/)) | CMS |
| Book delivery early | At least 2 full days before departure; no airport delivery | OxygenToGo |
| Read the cancellation terms | Full charges once shipped; non-use isn't refunded | OxygenToGo |

## Flying with a rental

- **Airline notice:** CAIRE's manual says to contact your carrier at least 48 hours before departure.
- **Batteries:** carry enough for at least **150%** of your expected travel time, including ground time, connections and delays (OxygenToGo, CAIRE, Inogen, OxyGo). OxygenToGo says you're responsible for bringing enough batteries.
- **Spares in the cabin:** extra batteries must be individually protected against short circuits and carried on.
- **FAA label:** the unit must meet FAA acceptance criteria. Rules are in [flying with a portable oxygen concentrator](/us/guides/faa-portable-oxygen-concentrator-rules/).

Use the [battery life tables](/us/guides/portable-oxygen-concentrator-battery-life/) to work out how many batteries you need. Example: a 6-hour door-to-door trip needs 9 hours of battery. On a Rove 6 at setting 2, that's two standard batteries (5 h each).

## At your destination

OxygenToGo says its rental units come with a car adaptor and wall power cord and accept up to 240 V, but you'll need a physical plug adapter abroad. Keep the unit away from open flames and smoking ([Inogen spec sheet](https://www.inogen.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/Inogen-Rove-6-Spec-Sheet.pdf): no open flames within 6.56 ft), and plug in whenever you can.

## Verdict

For a trip of a week or two, renting costs a fraction of buying. Budget about $400 to $700 plus shipping for a pulse unit at OxygenToGo's rates. Get your prescription and the right delivery type (pulse vs continuous) sorted first, book delivery 2 days early, tell your airline 48 hours ahead, and pack batteries for 150% of your journey. If you travel for many weeks a year, compare the total with buying.
