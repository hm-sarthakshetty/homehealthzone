## Short answer

The **Owgels 5L oxygen concentrator** is an older stationary unit with a published 0.5–5 LPM continuous-flow range, 90–95% listed oxygen purity, 350 W power draw, 19 kg weight and 50 dB sound claim. Those numbers describe a functional basic 5 LPM platform, but the model is marked **discontinued** in HHZ's current dataset and has no documented oxygen-purity indicator.

For a new purchase in 2026, current-production Home Medix and Oxymed models are more sensible where local support is proven. A used or old-stock Owgels is only worth considering after purity testing, hour/condition inspection and written spare-parts confirmation.

| OPI / purity analyser | Not documented |

The 5 LPM capacity is appropriate only when it matches the prescription. The weakness is monitoring: without a documented OPI or live analyser, the household cannot rely on the front panel to reveal purity deterioration. That increases the importance of periodic analyser testing by a competent service provider.

At 19 kg, the Owgels is movable on wheels but not genuinely portable. Its 50 dB claim is also above quieter current 5 LPM units, which matters in a bedroom during long daily use.

## New, old stock or refurbished?

Because this model is discontinued, every listing needs a stock-status check. Ask for the serial number and manufacturing/import date before payment. Confirm whether the unit is sealed fresh stock, old channel inventory, a demonstration unit or refurbished equipment.

For a used unit, require an oxygen-purity reading at 1 LPM and 5 LPM after at least 30 minutes of operation, inspect the flowmeter and alarms, and ask whether compressor and sieve-bed service is available locally. A cosmetic cleanup does not establish therapeutic performance.

## Owgels 5L versus current alternatives

The [Home Medix HM-KV](/oxygen-concentrators/home-medix-5-lpm/) is the stronger value alternative where authorised service is confirmed, with a lighter 13 kg chassis, lower field-verified sound, OPI plus live purity display, nebulization and a longer published warranty position. The [Oxymed Mini 5 LPM](/oxygen-concentrators/oxymed-mini-5-lpm/) is the alternative where its established local dealer and service route is stronger.

See the [Oxymed brand guide](/oxygen-concentrators/brands/oxymed/) if the similar names in Indian listings are causing confusion: **Owgels and Oxymed are different brands**. For a category-wide shortlist, use the [best 5 LPM concentrator ranking](/top-5/5-lpm-oxygen-concentrators/).

## Who might still consider it

A buyer may consider an Owgels 5L only when the price is substantially below a supported current-production unit, the prescription is within 0.5–5 LPM, measured purity is satisfactory, and a local technician can support the platform. It may also remain reasonable for an existing owner whose unit tests well and has a reliable repair route.

It is a poor choice for an oxygen-dependent user who cannot tolerate downtime, a buyer being charged current-model pricing, or anyone receiving vague answers about whether the machine is new.

## Verdict

The Owgels 5L is not automatically unusable because it is discontinued, but it is no longer the default purchase. Its missing OPI, 19 kg weight, 50 dB claim and uncertain stock history shift the burden of proof to the seller. Prefer a current, documented and locally serviceable 5 LPM machine unless the Owgels passes purity, condition and support checks at a compelling price.
