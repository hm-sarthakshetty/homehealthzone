# Portable Oxygen Concentrator Buyer's Guide (India 2026)

A portable oxygen concentrator (POC) concentrates oxygen from ambient air and is designed to be carried or moved. Some deliver pulses of oxygen as the user inhales; others also offer continuous flow. A battery-powered POC can support mobility, but the delivery mode and capacity must match the oxygen prescription. The [NHS explains prescribed home oxygen therapy](https://www.nhs.uk/tests-and-treatments/home-oxygen-treatment/).

## Pulse dose vs continuous flow

A pulse-dose machine senses inhalation and delivers a measured pulse; a continuous-flow machine delivers oxygen continuously. **A pulse setting of 2 is not the same as 2 litres per minute (LPM).** Settings and delivered amounts vary by device, so compare the actual mode and specification with the prescription rather than matching the number alone. An [FDA device summary](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf25/K252268.pdf) illustrates separate pulse settings and a continuous mode.

## What to check before buying

1. **Delivery:** Confirm whether the prescription calls for pulse or continuous flow and whether the device supports the required mode and range. Do not convert a pulse setting into LPM yourself.
2. **Battery:** Ask for the manufacturer's runtime at the required setting, breathing rate and battery configuration. Compare this with the time away from a power supply and allow for delays; check spare battery availability and recharge options.
3. **Practical carry weight:** Confirm whether the published weight includes the battery and case. Check the cannula connection, alarms and controls in person where possible.
4. **Travel and power:** Check the specific carrier's current rules for the exact device and batteries before booking. Verify the supplied charger for Indian mains and plan for power interruptions.
5. **Service:** Obtain the seller's written warranty, service address and availability of batteries and replacement parts. The [Home Medix dealer and service network](/in/respiratory-care-experts/) identifies partners by region and describes services including setup, mask fitting, rentals, repairs and staff training. Service availability varies by partner.

For a person who needs oxygen only at home, compare the capacity and running requirements of a [stationary oxygen concentrator](/oxygen-concentrators/) before paying for portability. For mobility, start with the prescribed delivery mode, then select a device with sufficient battery capacity and support for the intended journey.

## Battery duration and trip planning

A battery figure is meaningful only alongside its test conditions. Compare the delivery mode, setting, battery size, breathing rate and whether the battery is new. Runtime can vary with conditions and the device's battery condition; a headline “up to” number is not a promise at every setting.

The SimplyGo catalogue lists three hours at pulse setting 2. The [Philips user manual](https://www.documents.philips.com/assets/20180921/7441eb0a346744ddbe88a96200bc20ca.pdf) lists runtime at 20 breaths per minute and separate figures for continuous flow. These are published figures, not HHZ measurements. Verify the current manufacturer documentation and the particular battery supplied before planning around them.

G5 runtime depends on the pulse setting, breathing rate and battery configuration. Use [Inogen’s G5 manual](https://www.inogen.com/pdf/96-08649-00-01%20C%20G5%20User%20Manual-web.pdf) and current battery documentation to compare the configuration supplied. Ask the seller to identify whether the quote includes a single or larger battery and the runtime conditions used for its estimate.

For a journey, add time at the station or airport, transfers and possible delays to the expected period away from power. Check charging time and whether the supplied charger works from the power source available. Keep spare batteries charged and protected as directed by the manufacturer. A plan for interruptions matters even for short outings.

## Flying and other travel from India

Airline acceptance is specific to the carrier, route, device, batteries and planned use. Ask the carrier about advance notification, required documentation, the device label, cabin stowage, battery capacity and spare-battery limits before travel. Do not assume that a listing's “FAA approved” wording proves acceptance by every airline or on every flight.

For flights governed by US rules, the [FAA explains its POC acceptance criteria](https://www.faa.gov/about/initiatives/cabin_safety/portable_oxygen) and notes that the former individual approval process was replaced by criteria and labelling. Its [battery guidance for POCs](https://www.faa.gov/hazmat/packsafe/pocs) says to carry sufficient spare batteries for the flight and protect them against damage and short circuit. This US guidance is a reference, not a statement of Indian carrier policy.

For rail or road journeys, check the operator's current requirements for the device and power access. A berth or vehicle socket should not be assumed to supply continuous or suitable charging. Carry the device and power equipment in a way that keeps vents clear and allows the user to monitor alarms; follow the model's instructions.

## Weight, mode and everyday use

A shoulder-carried pulse-only unit and a heavier unit with continuous flow solve different problems. The G5 and SimplyGo comparison above is useful precisely because the latter lists both delivery modes but weighs more. Choose by the required delivery mode first, then assess how much weight the user or companion can carry along with the case and spare battery.

Try a normal route in the intended configuration: from the house to a vehicle, through a doorway, and over any stairs. Check the length and routing of the cannula, the readability of controls, and how a low-battery or fault alarm is conveyed. A wheeled cart may reduce carrying effort but also adds bulk to a journey.

A stationary concentrator can be appropriate for home use where mobility and battery operation are not priorities. A portable unit may be used alongside a home device when the user's plan calls for it, but owning two devices is not automatically necessary. Compare modes, capacity, power needs, service and the full cost of the intended arrangement.

## Costs and after-sales support in India

Request an itemised quote stating the device model, battery configuration, power supply, bag or cart, cannula, warranty and any delivery or setup charges. Compare availability and prices for genuine replacement batteries, filters and other model-specific parts. A cheap device quote may exclude equipment needed to use or transport it.

Ask where the unit would be assessed and repaired, what the warranty covers, and whether a temporary replacement is available during service. Retain the serial number and proof of purchase. ## Frequently asked questions

### Is a pulse setting equal to litres per minute?

No. A pulse setting identifies a device-specific delivery setting; continuous flow is expressed in LPM. Match the prescribed delivery mode and settings with the exact device rather than treating the numbers as equivalent.

### Does every portable concentrator offer continuous flow?

No. Some are pulse-only, while others include both modes. In the catalogue examples above, the G5 lists pulse settings and the SimplyGo lists both pulse and continuous flow.

### How many batteries are needed for a trip?

That depends on the device, battery size, operating conditions and time away from charging. Use the manufacturer's runtime information for the intended mode and setting, add a margin for delays, and check the carrier's current requirements for travel.

### Can a POC replace a stationary concentrator?

Some portable models may meet a user's needs, but portability alone does not establish that. Compare the required delivery mode and capacity, intended hours of use, battery and power arrangements, and the clinician's oxygen plan.

### Can I take any POC on a flight?

Do not assume so. Confirm the exact model, label, intended use and battery arrangements with each airline on the itinerary. Rules and operator procedures differ, and US FAA guidance does not automatically establish acceptance on an Indian carrier.

### Is the displayed price the total cost?

Not necessarily. The catalogue price is indicative. A battery upgrade, spare battery, case, delivery, supplies and service may change the final quote. Ask the seller to list the complete package and warranty in writing.
